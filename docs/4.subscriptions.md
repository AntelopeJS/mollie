Subscriptions are Mollie's own. Mollie keeps the schedule and creates each renewal payment against the subscription's mandate. This module maps the subscriptions interface onto Mollie's Subscriptions API, and reads Mollie's renewal payments back through the payment webhook.

## Creating

`CreateSubscription` checks the request with the interface's `ValidateSubscriptionRequest`, and refuses an interval that Mollie cannot bill (see [Intervals](#intervals)). Then it does the following:

1. It reads the payment method's mandate. The mandate must belong to the request's customer, be `valid`, and be usable without the payer. Otherwise the request is invalid (`InvalidSubscriptionRequestError`), and the module creates nothing.
2. It looks for a subscription of this customer that was created with the same idempotency key, and resumes it if there is one.
3. Otherwise it creates the Mollie subscription.
4. Without a trial or a billing anchor, it charges the first period itself and records the charge on the subscription.

### The first period

Mollie creates subscription payments on the schedule's dates, after the call that made the subscription has returned. The interface wants the first period charged before `CreateSubscription` resolves. So without a trial or an anchor, Mollie's schedule starts one period from today, and the module charges the first period as an ordinary recurring payment whose metadata names the subscription under the module's own key, `antelope.subscription`. That charge is the subscription's `latestPayment` until Mollie's first renewal.

Mollie settles that charge after the call returns (see [Payments](./2.payments.md)), so the subscription is created `active` with the charge still pending. If the charge later fails, the subscription is `past_due` and the module raises `subscription.payment_failed`. Mollie retries the payments it creates. Mollie does not know about this one, so **nothing retries a failed first charge**. The subscription stays `past_due` until Mollie's first renewal succeeds, or until you cancel it. If Mollie declines the charge in the creating response itself, the module cancels the subscription at Mollie and hides it from the interface, and `CreateSubscription` rejects with `SubscriptionPaymentError`. A replay of its key rejects the same way, and first cancels the subscription at Mollie if that cancel had failed. The module hides the subscription only once Mollie has canceled it. If the call fails before that, Mollie still bills the subscription, so the interface lists and reads it as `past_due`, with the declined charge as its latest payment. A replay of the key, or `CancelSubscription`, cancels it.

Only the call that receives Mollie's answer to the charge can tell a decline from a later failure. A replay that finds the first charge already failed reads the subscription again. If a call has recorded the charge by then, the replay returns the subscription, `past_due`. Otherwise it cancels the subscription at Mollie and rejects with `SubscriptionPaymentError`. If no call recorded the decline either, the cancelled subscription stays listed as `canceled`. That happens when the call that charged failed before it could record the decline, and also when it was still running: its record of the decline then reaches a subscription the replay already cancelled, which Mollie no longer lets anyone update. Mollie bills neither.

### Replays

Mollie keeps an idempotency key for one hour. `CreateSubscription` does not depend on that. The module stores the key on the subscription, so a replay at any later time finds the subscription instead of creating it again. The replay also finds the first charge among the customer's 250 most recent payments, instead of charging again.

A creation can fail partway, because of the network, Mollie or the process. **Replay a failed creation with the same key.** The Mollie subscription may already exist, and Mollie will charge it from the next period. A caller that gives up instead, or retries under a new key, leaves a subscription that charges and that no one asked for.

Replay only after the call you repeat has failed. A replay that runs while that call is still recording the first charge can cancel the subscription that call returns, if the charge fails in between. The subscription then stays listed, as `canceled`.

## Time

Mollie's `startDate` and `nextPaymentDate` are calendar dates, and Mollie charges at some point on that day. The module reads them as days in Europe/Amsterdam, because Mollie's documentation names no time zone:

- The module rounds a **trial end** or **billing anchor** up to the start of the next Amsterdam day, so nothing is charged before the time asked for. The subscription reports that rounded time as `trialEnd` and `currentPeriodEnd`. A time that already falls on an Amsterdam midnight stays as it is.
- A **period** runs from one Amsterdam midnight to the midnight of the next schedule date. Across a daylight-saving change, a period is an hour longer or shorter than whole days.
- Month periods keep the anchor's day of the month and clamp to the last day of a shorter month, as Mollie does ("we will charge on the last date of the month").

## Intervals

| Interface   | Mollie      | Longest |
| ----------- | ----------- | ------- |
| `day` × n   | `n days`    | 365     |
| `week` × n  | `n weeks`   | 52      |
| `month` × n | `n months`  | 12      |
| `year` × 1  | `12 months` | 1       |

The module refuses a longer period with `InvalidSubscriptionRequestError`. It stores the interval and its count with the subscription, so a year reads back as a year.

Anything holding the API key can change that stored interval. When it holds an interval or a count the module could not have written, such as a count of 0, -1, 1.5 or past the longest above, the subscription reads with the interval Mollie bills instead: `n days` as `day` × n, `n weeks` as `week` × n and `n months` as `month` × n. Mollie documents only these forms, within the longest above. If Mollie ever reports another, such as `0 days` or `1 year`, while the stored interval was changed too, the module cannot tell the period: `GetSubscription`, `CancelSubscription` and a replay of its `CreateSubscription` reject with a `SubscriptionError` that names the subscription, and `ListSubscriptions` leaves it out so the customer's other subscriptions still list. A stored cancellation end that is not a whole number of milliseconds from 1970 to the last day of 9999 drops the cancellation record, and the subscription then reads as ended when Mollie cancelled it.

## Statuses

| Situation                                                               | Status     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Mollie `active` or `pending`, before a trial ends, nothing charged      | `trialing` |
| Mollie `active` or `pending`, latest payment not failed                 | `active`   |
| Latest payment `failed`, `canceled` or `expired`, or Mollie `suspended` | `past_due` |
| Mollie `canceled` or `completed`                                        | `canceled` |

After a failed renewal, Mollie retries up to five times, then cancels the subscription. Some SEPA reasons cancel it at once (Mollie: _Recurring payments_). A subscription that Mollie ended that way reports `endReason: "payment_failed"`. A subscription ended by a cancellation, by revoking its mandate or by deleting the customer reports `"requested"`. At Mollie, revoking a mandate cancels every subscription that charges it, and deleting a customer cancels all of the customer's subscriptions.

`vendorData` holds the raw Mollie status, start date, next payment date and interval.

## Cancelling

Mollie cancels a subscription at once, and nobody can change a cancelled subscription afterwards. Before cancelling, the module records on the subscription how it was cancelled and under which idempotency key.

- **`now`.** The module cancels the subscription at Mollie. It reports `canceled`, with `endedAt` from Mollie and `endReason: "requested"`.
- **`period_end`.** The module cancels the subscription at Mollie at once, so nothing more is charged, and records it as ending when the current period ends. Until then the subscription reports its status and `cancelAtPeriodEnd: true`. From then on, it reports `canceled` with `endedAt` at that period end. Asking again is not an error.
- **`now` after `period_end`.** Mollie no longer accepts changes to the subscription, so the module records the early end on the customer, in the customer's metadata beside your own, and `GetCustomer` returns the same metadata before and after. Metadata another writer set keeps its keys. If that writer stored a string, a number or a list, which is not an object, the module moves the value into its own record, under `antelope.kept`, so Mollie still holds it but the other writer now finds an object. A string stays a string there even when it holds a JSON object, and `GetCustomer` reads it as it read it before. If that writer's object holds a `metadata` object of its own, the module marks its record `antelope.foreign`, so `GetCustomer` keeps returning the object's own string entries. Each subscription ended this way takes one entry of about 40 bytes, with its end time and a short hash of the idempotency key, and the module never removes it. Mollie keeps about 1 kB of metadata per customer, so a customer has room for about twenty such cancellations, fewer when the customer carries metadata of its own, and fewer still when the module has to move that metadata under `antelope.kept` or mark it `antelope.foreign`, which adds its size and a few bytes more. Before it writes, the module counts the UTF-8 bytes of the metadata that would result, and when they pass 1,024 it rejects `CancelSubscription` with a `SubscriptionError` that says the customer's metadata leaves no room to record the early end, and sends nothing: the customer's metadata stays as it is and the subscription keeps reporting its scheduled end. Free room by shortening the customer's own metadata, then cancel again. Mollie documents "approximately 1kB" and, in test mode, refused a write of 1,096 bytes with 422 "The metadata storage is currently limited to 1024 bytes" and accepted one of 1,000. Whether it counts bytes or characters, and what it does within a few bytes of 1,024, is not documented, so the module counts UTF-8 bytes against 1,024. Removing an entry would make that subscription report its scheduled end instead of its early one. Each early end rewrites the customer's metadata from a read just before it, and Mollie has no conditional update. So the module records one customer's early ends one at a time, but only within one process. Two processes, such as two replicas of your app, that end subscriptions of the same customer early at the same moment can still erase one of the two entries, and that subscription then reports its scheduled end.
- **Repeats.** A repeated cancellation under the same key returns the subscription. Under another key, on an ended subscription, it rejects with `InvalidSubscriptionStateError`.

Changing the payment method of a subscription set to end at period end rejects with `InvalidSubscriptionStateError`. Mollie will not change it, and nothing will be charged again.

## Events

| Event                            | Raised by                                                 | Event id                               |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| `subscription.created`           | `CreateSubscription`, and its replays                     | `<subscription>:subscription.created`  |
| `subscription.canceled`          | `CancelSubscription` (`now`, or `now` after `period_end`) | `<subscription>:subscription.canceled` |
| `subscription.payment_succeeded` | the webhook of a paid charge                              | `<subscription>:<payment>:paid`        |
| `subscription.payment_failed`    | the webhook of a failed, cancelled or expired charge      | `<subscription>:<payment>:<status>`    |
| `subscription.canceled`          | the webhook of a failed charge after Mollie gave up       | `<subscription>:subscription.canceled` |

Mollie sends no webhook about a subscription itself, only about its payments. That has three consequences:

- A call raises its own `created` or `canceled` event, to the handlers registered in that instance. A handler that throws makes the call reject. Replaying the call's idempotency key raises the event again, under the same id.
- **A `period_end` cancellation raises nothing when the period runs out.** Nothing happens at Mollie then, and this module has no clock. Read the subscription, or act on `cancelAtPeriodEnd` and `currentPeriodEnd` yourself.
- A subscription that Mollie cancels without a failed payment to report it, such as after a revoked mandate or a deleted customer, raises no `canceled` event.

A payment event carries the subscription as read when the module verifies the delivery, which can be later than the state the event is about.

## Metadata

Mollie keeps about 1 kB of metadata per subscription. The module's own record (reference, idempotency key, interval, first charge, cancellation) takes up to about 400 bytes of it. That leaves about 600 bytes for a subscription's own `metadata`. Mollie does not document what it does beyond that.

## Renewal payments

Mollie forwards a subscription's metadata to every payment it creates for it (Mollie: _Create subscription_, `metadata`). So a renewal reports the subscription's `reference` and the subscription's own metadata. The module ties a renewal to its subscription through the `subscriptionId` Mollie sets on the payment.

## Only this module's subscriptions

A subscription counts as this module's when it carries the module's own record in its metadata. Others on the account may come from the dashboard, a plugin or other code. The module does not list them, `GetSubscription` does not find them, and the interface cannot cancel or change them. They raise no events, and the module does not treat their payments as subscription charges.
